Cognitive leadership Lens and net assessment

What human cognition is worth. How leaders keep it.

Where human cognition remains a competitive advantage in the age of agentic AI, what it is worth and how leaders protect it. Evidence-based, updated monthly.

The lens Anna Ernst

AI is becoming a utility. Every company can rent the same intelligence at falling prices, so intelligence alone stops being a source of advantage. What stays scarce is what an organisation does with it: the problems it chooses, the context it brings, the calls it makes and the risks it refuses.

Agentic AI speeds this up. As agents take over execution, leadership moves to direction, oversight and the exceptions that carry the real risk. Companies that let their thinking drift to the machine gain speed and lose distinctiveness. Companies that keep it build an advantage competitors cannot buy.

Instant AI output+Passive trust=Automated groupthink
Cognitive leadership+Intentional friction=High-value Cognitive ROI

Never outsource the core

Delegate execution to agents. Keep strategy, context and decision rights with people. The value sits there. So does the liability.

Measure Cognitive ROI

Hours saved show up in every competitor's numbers. Cognitive ROI tracks what does not: better decisions, risks avoided and strategic bets others do not make.

Build the capacity to challenge

The scarce capability is a leadership team that can challenge its own systems: test the assumptions, weigh the tradeoffs and reject a convenient conclusion. It weakens when unused and has to be trained.

Technology scales execution. Leadership sets direction. The companies that win will be led by people who can still think independently of the systems they rely on.

A

Every human cognitive advantage has a half-life

Cognitive ROI treats each human cognitive capability as a business asset. An asset is valued by what it earns, how long it lasts and what it costs to keep.

1

What it earns

Human marginal value on the capability, times what is at stake in the decisions that use it. Measured as people with AI against AI alone, on the same task.

2

How long it lasts

The speed at which AI closes the gap on that capability. Some gaps close in one model generation. Some have not moved.

3

What it costs to keep

Training, practice and deliberate friction. A capability nobody practises loses its value before AI catches up. This is the automation paradox as a budget line.

The measure

Across 420 papers to October 2026, people with AI beat people alone (g = +0.53) but did worse than the better of the two alone (g = −0.35). In the 2024 analysis the combination helped only where people outperformed the AI.

The AI side

On ten domains of human cognition, AI went from 27% with GPT-4 to 57% with GPT-5. Weakest: long-term memory storage (0 of 10) and speed (3 of 10). No newer model scored on this scale as of 6 Nov 2026, so the AI side is likely understated.

Still unmeasured

Human marginal value and erosion for every capability. Executive control, metacognition, social cognition and judgement have no AI scoring on this scale.

One measure: human marginal value

The same task, done by AI alone and by a person with AI. The difference is what human cognition adds. Tracked as AI improves.

First evidence on the measure
Verified source

Two meta-analyses by the same team: 2024 (106 experiments, 370 effects) and an automated update from October 2026 (420 papers, 1,955 effects). Hedges' g with 95% confidence interval. Above zero, the person with AI did better.

What it shows: the 2026 update confirms the 2024 pattern. People with AI beat people alone. Against the better of the two working alone, the combination did worse on average. The gap did not close. In 2024 it helped where people outperformed the AI and hurt where the AI outperformed people. The 2026 update does not yet split results that way or by model generation.

Source: Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone, When combinations of humans and AI are useful, Nature Human Behaviour 8, 2024. Studies published January 2020 to June 2023. Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone, AutoAnalysis, arXiv 2610.42001, October 2026: search and coding done by AI agents, human audit of 30 papers per stage, live results at auto-analysis.dev. Readiness 7, with that caveat.

D8

Method

Written before any search. Rules change only with a dated note on this page.

How each mark is calculated
Balance
Standardised effect size (Hedges' g) of a person with AI against AI alone on the same task, pooled across included studies with a random-effects model. Below zero, AI alone does better. Above zero, the person with AI does better.
Trend
Change in the pooled effect across AI model generations, ordered by model release date.
Confidence
High: three or more studies at readiness 6 or above, pointing the same way. Moderate: two studies, or one at readiness 6 or above. Low: one study below readiness 6. None: no study.
Dating
Every AI result carries the model name and test date. Results older than 18 months stay in the trend and count at half weight in today's balance.
Search and screening
3seed sources included so far

The three seed sources were selected by hand as anchors. The systematic search adds studies with each monthly update and reports how many were found, screened, excluded and included. Sources: arXiv, SSRN, NBER, PubMed and peer-reviewed journals. Window: 2024 to 2026. A study is included only with a human baseline on the same task, a reported sample size and a named AI model with its date.

Evidence table

One row per study. Columns: study, design, sample size, task, capability, AI model and date, human baseline, effect size, readiness level, risk of bias, mark for H1, H0 and H2, coder.

D9 Limits

What this cannot tell you

  • Lab results may not hold in daily work.
  • Studies that find no effect are published less often.
  • Some AI tests may have leaked into training data, which inflates AI scores.
  • A capability with no studies shows no evidence. That is not a result in either direction.
  • A balance between groups says nothing about a single person.

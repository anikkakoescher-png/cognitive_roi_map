Cognitive ROI CheckAre your people and your AI in the right places?

Ten business situations. Two minutes. Your pattern, what to watch under pressure and the move.

For teams

The individual test

  1. Pick the decisions that carry value.Pricing, credit, forecasts, product bets, incident response.
  2. Run each one three ways.The person alone, AI alone and the person with AI, on the same cases.
  3. Score every person.Result with AI minus AI alone. That is the person's human marginal value.
  4. Look at the spread.The average tells you whether to automate. The top of the spread tells you whom to keep in the loop.
  5. Profile the top.Measure the capabilities that separate them: calibration, problem formulation, metacognition, domain depth.
  6. Repeat with every model generation.The line moves. Re-test before each rollout decision.
Method

A row needs one study that compares at least two of the three on the same task: the person alone, the person with AI and AI alone. The row is labelled by which scored highest. Level: the study reports the leaders as about equal. Every row names the model and the year. Newer models are likely stronger, so older rows probably understate AI.

Limits
  • Most tasks are vignettes or lab tasks, not daily work.
  • Lay participants and experts are mixed across rows.
  • In venture selection, judgements were combined after the fact.
  • The coding and consulting studies used AI tools from 2023 and early 2025.
  • Most domains have no study yet. Legal work, coding, strategy, negotiation and research design are not measured.

Updated with each monthly review. Rows marked preprint are not yet peer reviewed.

Cognitive leadership Lens and net assessment

What human cognition is worth. How leaders keep it.

Every human cognitive advantage has a half-life. AI closes some gaps within one model generation and leaves others untouched. This page measures which human capabilities still add value next to AI, what they are worth and what it costs to keep them. Cognitive ROI brings the three together for each capability.

The lens Anna Ernst

AI is becoming a utility. Every company can rent the same intelligence at falling prices, so intelligence alone stops being a source of advantage. What stays scarce is what an organisation does with it: the problems it chooses, the context it brings, the calls it makes and the risks it refuses.

Agentic AI speeds this up. As agents take over execution, leadership moves to direction, oversight and the exceptions that carry the real risk. Companies that let their thinking drift to the machine gain speed and lose distinctiveness. Companies that keep it build an advantage competitors cannot buy.

Instant AI output+Passive trust=Automated groupthink
Cognitive leadership+Intentional friction=High-value Cognitive ROI

Never outsource the core

Delegate execution to agents. Keep strategy, context and decision rights with people. The value sits there. So does the liability.

Measure Cognitive ROI

Hours saved show up in every competitor's numbers. Cognitive ROI tracks what does not: better decisions, risks avoided and strategic bets others do not make.

Build the capacity to challenge

The scarce capability is a leadership team that can challenge its own systems: test the assumptions, weigh the tradeoffs and reject a convenient conclusion. It weakens when unused and has to be trained.

Technology scales execution. Leadership sets direction. The companies that win will be led by people who can still think independently of the systems they rely on.

A

Every human cognitive advantage has a half-life

Cognitive ROI treats each human cognitive capability as a business asset. An asset is valued by what it earns, how long it lasts and what it costs to keep.

1

What it earns

Human marginal value on the capability, times what is at stake in the decisions that use it. Measured as people with AI against AI alone, on the same task.

2

How long it lasts

The speed at which AI closes the gap on that capability. Some gaps close in one model generation. Some have not moved.

3

What it costs to keep

Training, practice and deliberate friction. A capability nobody practises loses its value before AI catches up. This is the automation paradox as a budget line.

Every name for AI carries a picture of who decides and who answers for the result. Cognitive linguistics calls these pictures conceptual metaphors. Thirty-one names in use today fall into seven pictures.

A chatbot introduced as a "trained professional" won more trials. After use, the same chatbot introduced as a "toddler" was rated more usable and more likely to be adopted. Rollout returns depend on what happens after the trial.

The theory

Four ways a name works on the mind

1

Frame

A name opens a whole scene. "Copilot" brings a cockpit, a captain and a chain of command. People reason inside that scene without noticing it.

Crime described as a "beast" or a "virus" shifted which policies readers chose. A larger follow-up found the effect smaller and dependent on conditions.

Thibodeau and Boroditsky 2011; Steen et al. 2014; Fillmore 1982; Lakoff and Johnson 1980
2

Stance

"Agent", "teammate" and "brain" invite people to predict the system by its goals and beliefs. "Tool" and "engine" invite prediction by its design.

Shanahan argues that words such as knows, believes and thinks mislead about what language models do.

Dennett 1987; Shanahan, Communications of the ACM 2024
3

Social response

People apply social rules to computers on minimal cues. A human name or role is a strong cue.

Three factors predict how readily people see a mind in a machine: what they know about it, their need to explain it and their need for contact.

Weizenbaum 1966; Reeves and Nass 1996; Epley, Waytz and Cacioppo 2007
4

Calibration

Trust should track capability. Too much trust leads to misuse. Too little leads to disuse. A name sets where trust starts.

This is the classic account of trust in automation, written for cockpits and control rooms long before language models.

Parasuraman and Riley 1997; Lee and See 2004
The evidence on AI names

Five studies test what framing changes

Experiment, 2020

The same chatbot was introduced as a "toddler" or a "trained professional". The professional got more people to try it. After use, people rated the low-competence version more usable and were more likely to adopt it.

Khadpe et al., CSCW 2020, three experiments
Experiment, preprint

People shown language models as companions credited them with more mental capacities than people shown machines or tools. The effect held in a second study with 604 people. Effects on reliance were mixed.

Chen et al., arXiv 2025, revised 2026, 470 and 604 people
Experiment, 2024

People saw a human-like or a neutral description of an AI system. Human-like wording did not raise trust overall. The product and the stakes mattered more.

Inie et al., ACM FAccT 2024, 954 people
Experiment, preprint

People read texts about AI with or without human-like language. Their views of AI barely moved. A text about the dangers of AI did move them.

Hou et al., arXiv 2026, 815 people
Survey, 2026

People who saw AI as a teammate reported higher team cohesion, linked to their trust in it. The assigned framing did not take hold, so this is a correlation.

Hoffmann et al., Research for People in Organizations 2026, 217 people
Where science disagrees

Whether mind words fit at all is an open question

No meaning from form alone

A system trained only on text form cannot learn meaning.

Bender and Koller, ACL 2020
Prediction without explanation

Language models describe and predict. They do not explain, which human thought does.

Chomsky, Roberts and Watumull, New York Times 2023
A split field

Researchers disagree on whether these systems understand. Both sides have serious arguments.

Mitchell and Krakauer, PNAS 2023
For the boardroom. When scientists cannot agree that a word fits, a company that uses it takes a position. Staff and mind words claim the most.
Reading. Names set expectations before use. In the one study that followed people through use, the name that promised less won the verdict. Human-like wording alone did not raise trust in two large studies. So the name matters most at first contact. What people then see the system do decides the rest. Five studies, none of them in a workplace over time.

The measure

Across 420 papers to October 2026, people with AI beat people alone (g = +0.53) but did worse than the better of the two alone (g = −0.35). In the 2024 analysis the combination helped only where people outperformed the AI.

The AI side

On ten domains of human cognition, AI went from 27% with GPT-4 to 57% with GPT-5. Weakest: long-term memory storage (0 of 10) and speed (3 of 10). No newer model scored on this scale as of 6 Nov 2026, so the AI side is likely understated.

Still unmeasured

Human marginal value and erosion for every capability. Executive control, metacognition, social cognition and judgement have no AI scoring on this scale.

Method

Written before any search. Rules change only with a dated note on this page.

How each mark is calculated
Balance
Standardised effect size (Hedges' g) of a person with AI against AI alone on the same task, pooled across included studies with a random-effects model. Below zero, AI alone does better. Above zero, the person with AI does better.
Trend
Change in the pooled effect across AI model generations, ordered by model release date.
Confidence
High: three or more studies at readiness 6 or above, pointing the same way. Moderate: two studies, or one at readiness 6 or above. Low: one study below readiness 6. None: no study.
Dating
Every AI result carries the model name and test date. Results older than 18 months stay in the trend and count at half weight in today's balance.

E9 Limits

What this cannot tell you

  • Lab results may not hold in daily work.
  • Studies that find no effect are published less often.
  • Some AI tests may have leaked into training data, which inflates AI scores.
  • A capability with no studies shows no evidence. That is not a result in either direction.
  • A balance between groups says nothing about a single person.

The thesis

One core claim. Three findings that sharpen it.

Core
Human cognition gains value as AI advances.

Open. On tasks with a right answer, AI alone was ahead in 5 of 10 studies. The person alone was ahead in 2. The core claim stands or falls on what is not yet measured: innovation, problem finding and strategy.

Open
SupportedThe value sits in the spread.

The same AI made high performers better and low performers worse. Who the person is decides whether AI lifts or lowers the result.
